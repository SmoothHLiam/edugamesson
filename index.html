Ossa

Label the skeleton

Ossa is a labelling game for the human skeleton. Students name a highlighted bone, choose from four options, or find a named bone on the figure. The Core set is the 26 bones on a class master sheet. Complete goes to 48 and splits the cranium, ribs, wrist, ankle and pelvis into their parts.

02 Approach

Practice that asks before it tells.

The games follow four rules.

  1. 01

    Ask first.

    Students produce the answer before they see any options. Ossa leads with typing and offers multiple choice for when a student needs it.

  2. 02

    Explain every miss.

    After each answer the game shows the correct name and a one-line note, so a wrong answer still teaches something.

  3. 03

    Follow the syllabus.

    Content comes from a reference sheet, not a guess at what will be on the test. Ossa's Core set matches a class master sheet bone for bone.

  4. 04

    Keep sessions short.

    A run takes a few minutes. Students can stop at any point, then start again with only the bones they missed.

04 Questions

Common questions.

Is it free?

Ossa is free to play. There are no accounts, ads or paywalls.

Does it work on phones and tablets?

Yes. The layout adapts to small screens, and the figure zooms with a scroll wheel, a pinch or the on-screen buttons.

What happens to student data?

There are no accounts, so nothing identifies a student. Settings and best scores are saved only in the browser on that device. Clearing the site's data removes them.

Which bones does Ossa cover?

Core is the 26 bones on a class master sheet, from the cranium to the phalanges of the foot. Complete goes to 48 and splits the cranium, ribs, wrist, ankle and pelvis into their parts.

Will there be more games?

More titles are planned. Each will keep the same format: one subject, short sessions and no account.
